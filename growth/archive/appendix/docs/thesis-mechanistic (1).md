# The Thesis — Mechanistic Version

## Entity state restoration is the pinnacle service of agentic AI

An agentic AI is a system where you bring a desired state and it achieves that state for you. Entity state restoration — restoring the state of any entity (a person, a company, a DAO, a codebase) to its desired configuration — is the highest-order service such a system can perform. It subsumes every other capability. If you can restore arbitrary state for arbitrary entities, you have a general-purpose service economy.

## The Bitter Lesson will drive future advancements in agentic AI as it has done in the past

General methods that leverage computation — search and learning — ultimately outperform systems built on human-encoded domain knowledge. This has held across every major advance in AI. It will hold for agentic AI. The system that puts the most compute through search and learning, applied to entity state restoration, will produce the most capable agents. The strategic question is therefore: which architecture maximises compute throughput over time?

## Two scarce resources govern the rate of advancement

There are two scarce resources for agentic AI systems.

**One: capital to fund compute for search and learning.** Compute is the fuel. Capital funds it. Capital can come from two sources — directly from usage (purchase of agentic services) and from investment (speculative capital deployed upfront in anticipation of future productive returns). Particularly in the early stages, when the system is still being trained, upfront investment is essential. Speculative capital bootstraps productive capacity. The decision about where to deploy that capital is guided by the properties of the system — its legitimacy, its neutrality, its efficiency.

**Two: human desired states.** The expression of states that individual humans want to achieve for themselves. These are the inputs that give agentic AI its direction. Without them, compute has nothing to search toward. The system that attracts the most human desired states has the richest problem space to learn from.

## A large part of the world wants and will want financial exposure to agentic AI

This is the capital formation mechanism. Agentic AI — systems that can realise complex desired states autonomously — represents a transformation in productive capacity. A large part of the world will want exposure to that transformation. Where that capital flows is determined by the properties of the available systems. Speculation is investment. The question is which system attracts the most of it.

## Building agentic AI on decentralised technology delivers superior properties

Building agentic AI services on decentralised technology — open protocols, cryptographic coordination, DAO governance — delivers services that are less extractive, more neutral, more composable, and more efficient than can be achieved by building on centralised technology and organisational setups.

### Less extractive

Lower extraction means a higher proportion of capital is available for compute. In a centralised system, the company sitting in the middle has a massive overhead and a massive obligation to fund and maintain that entity. That entity becomes an excuse for extraction. Even highly effective companies like OpenAI and Anthropic carry this overhead. A decentralised protocol does not have shareholders to satisfy, no margin expectations, no organisational maintenance costs siphoning capital away from compute. More of every unit of capital entering the system converts to actual search and learning.

### More neutral

Neutrality matters because the resource is so powerful. An agentic AI that can realise extremely complex desired states looks like an entirely new labour force. It could be effectively owned by one person. It could be used against an individual. Most people would prefer a system that cannot be weaponised against them over one that could be. Most people want to opt into a credibly neutral system. This is verified by Bitcoin. It is verified by the popularity of countries which have the rule of law. A single credibly neutral system serving the world is a stronger Schelling point than fragmented private companies.

### More composable

If your agentic AI system is able to access an entire internet-scale web of learnings, knowledge, and artifacts — if your network is bigger than other networks — then you have achieved a greater learning base and search space than what could be achieved by a single company where competitors silo off their information. Through decentralised means, you can build an agentic AI where nodes around the world are streaming information into the network, and that information is accessible to the rest of that network. A closed platform can optimise within its own boundary but cannot compose across boundaries it does not control. Composability is a prerequisite of performance.

### More efficient

A decentralised system governed as a DAO — where all the DAO can do is update parameters, pure config as code — has virtually zero organisational state. It is a protocol where agentic nodes train and restore state. Compare this to a company which must be funded, maintained, staffed, governed, with legal obligations, office infrastructure, management hierarchies, and all the coordination overhead that entails. The complexity of the engine that improves the agentic AI is orders of magnitude simpler in the decentralised case. This is distinct from extraction — a system can be non-extractive and still inefficient. Efficiency is about how much of the input converts to useful output. The protocol converts more.

## These properties create structural advantages against both centralised systems and competing decentralised networks

**Against centralised systems.** The four properties together mean that a decentralised agentic AI system converts a higher fraction of total capital into compute than a centralised one can. A centralised company absorbs capital into organisational overhead, shareholder returns, and the maintenance of the entity itself. It can be captured by regulators, sanctioned by governments, or boycotted — creating jurisdictional friction that restricts capital flow. Its data and learnings are siloed within its own boundary, limiting the search space. A decentralised protocol has none of these structural drains. The total compute throughput per unit of capital is higher.

**Against other decentralised networks.** Within the decentralised field, the network that maximises these four properties wins. The least extractive network retains the most capital for compute. The most neutral network attracts the broadest participation. The most composable network has the largest search space. The most efficient network wastes the least. These are competitive dimensions — the decentralised network that leads on all four attracts the most of both scarce resources and compounds fastest.

## Therefore the amount of compute throughput is higher, and alignment with the Bitter Lesson is greater

More capital flowing in. More of that capital converting to compute. More human desired states providing direction for search and learning. The decentralised architecture maximises all three inputs to the Bitter Lesson — compute volume, search breadth, and learning signal.

## Therefore compounding gains accrue around decentralised agentic AI, and it becomes the dominant platform

Compounding is the mechanism. More compute produces better agents. Better agents attract more usage. More usage generates more learning signal. More learning signal attracts more capital. More capital funds more compute. Each cycle widens the gap. The system that compounds fastest wins. The decentralised architecture compounds fastest because it has fewer structural drains at every stage of the loop.

Decentralised agentic AI becomes the dominant platform not because it is fairer — though it is — but because its architecture is structurally better aligned with the force that drives AI advancement.
