## Context

PR velocity on `Jinn-Network/mono` has outgrown serial manual merging. Merged-PR volume into `next` ran 7–10/day over 2026-08-16→18 and 13.6/day (peak 34) under the July Autopilot regime, against a CI battery whose longest lane runs 52–73 minutes.

The motivating incident is the **Stage-6 train** (2026-08-17): eight stacked PRs merged in 4.7 minutes, `next` gate-red for roughly eight hours, three fix-forward PRs (#2749, #2753, #2755) to recover. That class of event — untested merge results landing on the integration branch — recurs roughly weekly. (The [#2786](https://github.com/Jinn-Network/mono/pull/2786) conflict loop, initially considered as the motivator, does not survive scrutiny: two of its three reconciles were paid before it was reviewable, and it sat at zero reviews — its binding constraint was review latency, which a queue does not touch. This DR makes the case on Stage-6, not #2786.)

Ground truths from the research lanes and the two pre-presentation reviews:

1. **`next` has zero required status checks today.** No classic branch protection exists; the "Base" ruleset (15714818, covering `~DEFAULT_BRANCH` + `main` + `next` — note `next` *is* the default branch) requires one approving review plus code-owner review and blocks deletion/force-push — nothing else. All CI green/red is advisory. Enabling a merge queue therefore *creates the mechanical gate layer for the first time*. The repo has already codified its intent: `.github/scripts/branch-protection-audit.mjs` names `platform-architecture-control` and `platform-verification` as required contexts, and the scheduled `Architecture Policy Audit` fails because live state never caught up (it also still reads the classic-protection API, which rulesets do not populate, and audits a branch — `integration/evidence-v1` — that no longer exists).
2. **GitHub applies one required-context list to both PRs and merge groups.** Passing required checks is what makes a PR enqueueable; the same contexts must then pass on the speculative merge commit. A required workflow that does not report on `merge_group` leaves the context unreported; after the check-response timeout, unreported checks are treated as failed and the entry is ejected. Zero workflows in this repo declare `merge_group` today, so naive enablement ejects 100% of entries. The hazard is finer-grained than triggers: several current workflows read `github.event.pull_request.*`, `github.base_ref`, or `github.event.before`, which are empty on `merge_group` — one would error (`Canonical docs check`), one would silently skip its main comparison (`Stack fixture immutability`), and `jinn-agent CI` gates every job on `event_name == pull_request || push`, so a bare trigger line would make it report green having run nothing. Pre-wiring is therefore an **event-context audit**, not a trigger sprinkle.
3. **Merge limits do not combine CI builds** — under the all-green strategy every queue entry gets its own speculative merge commit and its own full battery regardless of landing batch size; batching only groups the final ref-moves ("Merge limits do not combine `merge_group` builds. Merge limits only affect merges to the base branch once one or more `merge_group` has satisfied build checks." — GitHub docs). What that sentence does **not** establish is whether speculative successors survive a landing ref-move under `max_entries_to_merge=1`; no public production account of that configuration was found. D5 treats it as a to-be-verified mechanic, not a fact.
4. **The runner ceiling is the binding constraint, not flakes.** The org is on the free plan (20 concurrent GitHub-hosted jobs; no self-hosted runners). A full merge-group battery is on the order of 60–100 jobs once platform-verification's eight domain lanes fan out. Speculative build concurrency multiplies that, and the check-response timeout is wall-clock — jobs queued behind the concurrency ceiling burn timeout budget while idle, and ejections re-enqueue and add load. D5's concurrency choice is made against this ceiling, not against an idealized parallel battery.
5. **Merge-group batteries are more expensive than PR batteries.** `Platform Architecture Control` bypasses its changed-package selection on any non-`pull_request` event and verifies in full (observed full runs 11–43 min vs 1 min unselected), and the operator `CI` workflow's declared worst-case path (`check` ≈30 min → `task-creator-amd64-gold-proof`, timeout 90 min) is 120 minutes. The realistic merge-group battery is ~45–75 min typical with a declared worst case of ~120 min — the timeout in D5 is set against that distribution, not the PR-lane one.
6. Measured flake ≈10% of full batteries in the sampled window, concentrated in two open issues — [#2766](https://github.com/Jinn-Network/mono/issues/2766) and [#2782](https://github.com/Jinn-Network/mono/issues/2782), both in Benchmark Product CI. One standing cost the queue removes for free: burst merges to `next` cancel each other's `hermetic-gate` runs (13 of 30 recent push runs, 43%) because its concurrency group keys on `refs/heads/next`; queue refs are unique per entry. And today's burst landings race the unserialized SDK-publish chain (no `concurrency:` block) — spacing landings a battery apart is a strict improvement to canary integrity, not a regression.

## Decisions

**D1 — Engine: GitHub-native merge queue.** Configured via a repository-level ruleset on `next` (the `merge_queue` rule is repo-level-only). Free for this repo's shape (public, org-owned), no third-party app with write access, and the enqueue mutation's `expectedHeadOid` natively expresses the exact-head discipline rule 4 already runs on. **Named escalation path:** Mergify's OSS tier ($0 for public repos) — considered only if flake-driven throughput collapse persists *after* #2766/#2782 are closed and quarantine discipline is in place. That trigger condition, not vendor preference, is what would reopen the engine choice.

**D2 — Scope: `next` only; no actor retains direct push to `next`.** `main` stays entirely outside the queue: its write paths (`promote-main.yml` fast-forward, hotfix squash-merges under `Main base guard`, the standing release-review PR) are untouched. Because `bypass_actors` is a **ruleset-level** array and `main` + `next` currently share the "Base" ruleset, the flip **splits it**: a `main` ruleset retaining the current rules *and* the admin bypass (`promote-main.yml`'s `RELEASE_PROMOTION_TOKEN` push and hotfix operations depend on it), and a `next` ruleset carrying the pull-request rule, the required-check rule, the `merge_queue` rule, **empty `bypass_actors`**, and `dismiss_stale_reviews_on_push: true`. Dismiss-stale is not new policy: the audit script has always asserted it, and rule 4(c) / CLAUDE.md already promise that a mutated head "re-enters every review and merge gate" — this setting is what makes that promise (and D4's invariant, for humans as well as agents) mechanical rather than conventional. The hotfix **back-merge** (runbook step 9, today a direct `git push origin next` that works only via admin bypass) becomes a PR through the queue — the runbook's existing conflict-fallback shape (`chore: back-merge hotfix v<x.y.z>`) becomes the only shape. By step 9 the hotfix has already shipped to `@latest` (step 8), so the back-merge is repo hygiene, not the emergency; its honest cost is one battery **plus a code-owner approval** (recorded under a non-author operator credential per rule 4's plumbing note; a `main`→`next` merge touches code-owned paths essentially by construction), and during that window `main-next-ancestor-check` will hold its self-healing `[main-next-divergence]` alert open — expected, documented, auto-closes on landing. Break-glass for a wedged queue (GitHub incident, all-entries-failing) is an **auditable ruleset edit** — an admin disables the `merge_queue` rule, lands the critical PR through the still-required checks, re-enables, and immediately `workflow_dispatch`es the policy audit to confirm restoration (the audit's weekly cron is a backstop, not the forcing function; the re-enable + dispatch steps are the runbook's forcing function). Never a silent push.

**D3 — Required-check architecture: a product-covering explicit set.** The initial required contexts are **target names, not existing check names** — today these strings name workflows (and in `hermetic-gate`'s case a script-posted check-run), while several current job names collide repo-wide (`architecture` ×14, `verify` ×12, `Build and test` in two different workflows). Step 0 therefore gives each member a **uniquely-named terminal gate job** (`needs:` + `if: always()` + explicit result assertions — the exact anti-vacuous-pass construction `platform-architecture-control.yml` already ships in its `platform-verification` gate job, generalized), and a test asserts required-context uniqueness **within the required set** plus correct `merge_group` handling. The set:

- `platform-architecture-control` and `platform-verification` (the audit script's codified intent). On `merge_group`, PAC bypasses changed-package selection and verifies in full — conservative and correct day one; teaching `verification-selection` to diff the merge-group payload (`base_sha`/`head_sha` exist in the event) is a named optimization with real value, since it converts the 11–43-min full run back into the cheap selected case for narrow entries. **Consequence acknowledged:** platform-verification's eight domain lanes ride in through PAC on every merge group — so `Task Execution CI`'s domain lane *is* exercised on the required path (in the canary-lane profile measured inside the 11–43-min PAC envelope). What stays excluded is the *standalone* Task Execution CI context with its observed 253-minute outlier runs; those outliers must be bounded before that standalone context could ever be required. Platform-verification also mints sigstore attestations per run; on merge groups these bind to speculative SHAs (including ejected entries) — a known, accepted side effect, noted in the enacting issue.
- `hermetic-gate` — an API-posted check-run (`post-check-run-verdict.mjs`) bound to `github.sha`, which on `merge_group` is the speculative merge commit — correct, but verified explicitly in step 0, not assumed. Making it required fulfills the two-gate redesign's own migration intent; its in-file "intentionally NOT a required check yet" note and the classic-API `enable-hermetic-gate-required.sh` / `enable-main-base-guard.sh` precedent scripts are retired or ported in the same motion (running a classic-API script after a rulesets flip would create a second, overlapping protection layer). One asymmetry accepted knowingly: `JINN_HERMETIC_GATE_WAIVED` can waive the *publish* guard but nothing waives a required queue context.
- The operator product surface: operator `CI`, `Operator console CI`, `Layer CI`, `jinn-agent CI` — because rule 8 ships an operator canary from pushes to `next`, and a queue that attests platform packages while the operator product lands broken fails the honesty test (Stage-6's tip reds were on exactly these surfaces). These four are paths-filtered today; step 0 **converts workflow-level `paths:` filters to job-level conditions** (GitHub's documented-safe pattern: a skipped *job* reports success; a skipped *workflow* hangs the queue): on `pull_request` the jobs keep today's path scoping — PR-lane cost stays what it is now — and on `merge_group` they run unconditionally. `jinn-agent CI`'s per-job `event_name` gates are extended to `merge_group` as part of the event-context audit, without which it would report vacuously green.
- The deterministic pair: `Repository structure`, `Stack fixture immutability` (≈0% failure, <8 min) — the latter gains a `merge_group` branch for its base-ref resolution (today it falls through `github.event.before` to a silent skip on non-PR events, which a required context must not do).
- `Canonical docs check` — required, with an explicit **merge-group no-op**: its subject is the PR body (the Discussion link), which is immutable-relevant at enqueue time and already enforced by the PR-side run that gated enqueue; on `merge_group` the gate job exits green with a notice. Running its current script on a merge group would error (`github.base_ref` empty) and, guarded naively, would hard-fail every group containing a canonical-doc change.

**Named exclusions, and the honest coverage statement:** `environment-suite` permanently (release gate; its global ref-independent concurrency group would serialize the queue). Standalone `Task Execution CI` and `Benchmark Product CI` until, respectively, outlier-bounding and #2766/#2782 closure — with the note above that task-execution *content* already rides the required path via platform-verification. **Not covered by the v1 set** (advisory PR-side and push-to-`next` only, breakage landable through the queue): `contracts/`, `packages/indexer/**`, `apps/website/`, and the benchmark-product surface — named as accepted v1 gaps with the same admission path, rather than claimed as covered. The required workflows must never carry workflow-level `paths:` filters; all selection is job-level. **Tiered lanes (operator ruling, 2026-08-18, same session):** the PR lane runs an event-conditional **fast mode** under the same context names — per-area typecheck and unit suites; no verification fan-out, no e2e, no pack smokes — and the queue runs the **full battery with diff-aware selection**: PAC's `verification-selection` and the operator-surface workflows' job-level conditions evaluate the merge-group diff (`base_sha`/`head_sha` are in the event payload) with the same conservatism the PR lane already trusts (root-level changes run everything). Narrow entries drop from the full battery to minutes, which also relieves the runner ceiling in Context #4. The accepted cost, ruled explicitly: integration failures surface at queue time, so ejection churn rises until #2766/#2782 close; D5's one-free-re-enqueue policy is the containment.

**D4 — Review and merge authority: credential-bound; the queue merges; self-enqueue permitted.** *(Amended by operator ruling, 2026-08-18, same session.)* The session's original ruling re-expressed the Autopilot merge carve-out as enqueue-only. A same-day operator ruling went further and retired the species framing: in this repo, reviews are performed by agents operating under operator credentials — Autopilot runs with the operator's own credentials — so "no agent self-merge" and "human-surface PRs wait for a human" drew a line the mechanics never enforced. Rule 4 is rewritten in full: **review is a pipeline stage, not an identity ritual.** Every PR receives an agent review pass and carries an approving review from the **operator credential set** before it can enqueue; the control surface is the credential set, with CODEOWNERS paths requiring a code-owner credential. A third same-day ruling sharpened this: **credential distinctness is not doctrine** — one operator's agent reviewing another operator's PR and the same operator's agent reviewing it are the same act, so the rule claims no independence from the reviewer's identity. GitHub separately refuses to record an approval from the PR's own authoring account, so approvals land under a non-author operator credential; the rule names that as platform plumbing, nothing more. **Self-enqueue is permitted:** once review and required checks are green, any operator credential, including the author's, may enqueue. This is safe *because of* the queue: enqueue carries no discretion — the recorded approval, the code-owner credential gate, and the tested landing are all machine-enforced, so the author-must-not-merge separation protected nothing the machine does not already protect. The queue remains the only merger; Autopilot pins enqueue to the exact reviewed head (`enqueuePullRequest(expectedHeadOid)`, never `gh pr merge --auto` — repo-level `allow_auto_merge` is `false`, and auto-merge-under-rulesets carries known reliability problems besides). The invariant: *the approved head equals the PR head at enqueue time; the landed commit is the queue-constructed merge whose PR-side parent is that exact head; every required check passed on the byte-identical commit that lands.* Approval freshness stays mechanical via D2's `dismiss_stale_reviews_on_push: true` — a post-approval push dismisses the approval, so no head can enqueue without an approval given against it; the reconcile-child cost lands where rule 4 already placed it (the mutated head re-enters review — previously a promise, now a mechanism). This remains strictly stronger than the pre-queue carve-out: today's flow merges a reviewed head whose green checks may be stale against a moved base; the queue tests the actual integration result before landing it. **DR-2026-06-03 is superseded in part** (see header): its mechanism — CODEOWNERS + require-code-owner-review — survives as the credential gate; its doctrine that an agent approval never satisfies the code-owner gate is retired, because an agent operating under a code-owner credential satisfies it by design, not by loophole. The reconcile-children clause is unchanged apart from the `update-branch` retirement (the queue's speculative merge supersedes staleness-handling; `file-reconcile-child` survives for true conflicts), with escalation reworded from "a human" to "the operator". CLAUDE.md's "draft before mutation / re-enters every gate" sentence survives verbatim.

**D5 — Batching and failure policy: land one at a time, all-green, merge commits — with the load-bearing mechanic named as unverified.** Queue configuration: `merge_method=MERGE` (matches current practice; the 2026-04-23 GitHub queue incident affected squash and rebase, not plain merge), `grouping_strategy=ALLGREEN` (per-entry attribution; no bisection exists or is needed), **`max_entries_to_merge=1`** with `min_entries_to_merge=1` — every PR lands as its own push, so rule 8's per-PR `canary.<sha>` cadence survives as written (noting the cadence was always "pushes that touch the publish paths": `sdk-npm-publish.yml` is paths-filtered and `npm-publish.yml` rides its `workflow_run` with a `branches: [next]` filter — which is also why nothing publishes from `gh-readonly-queue/*` refs). **The open mechanic:** GitHub's documentation establishes that batching doesn't share builds, but does *not* state whether speculative successors survive a landing ref-move under `max_entries_to_merge=1`, and no production account of this configuration was found. If successors are invalidated on each landing, throughput degrades toward one landing per battery (~45–75 min) — workable at the 2/day mean, painful at the 15/day peak. Accordingly: the flip's watch window runs a **scripted verification** (enqueue several trivial PRs; observe push events, successor survival, and rebuild behavior) **before Autopilot resumes enqueueing**; if the mechanic fails, the named fallback is default batching (`max_entries_to_merge=5`), which changes canary granularity to per-group and therefore **would amend rule 8** via its own docs PR — the one place this DR's "does not amend" is conditional. `max_entries_to_build=2` initially — chosen against the free-plan 20-concurrent-job ceiling, where a single battery already queues internally (~60–100 jobs); higher concurrency multiplies idle-in-queue time that the wall-clock check-response timeout charges against entries. Tunable 1–5 in the watch window on evidence. `check_response_timeout_minutes=180` — above the ~120-min declared worst-case battery path (operator CI's gold-proof tail) plus runner-queueing headroom, far below a day. **Flake policy:** an ejected entry may be re-enqueued once without new commits (that re-run is the measurement); a second ejection requires a filed fix-or-quarantine issue before re-entry. Enforcement is **mechanized on the agent path** — the enqueue-sweep (enacting item 4) tracks ejections per PR head and refuses a second re-enqueue without a linked issue — and **convention on the human path**, with ejections visible in PR timelines; a `merge_group.destroyed` webhook consumer is watch-window tooling in enacting item 2, not a promise this DR makes for day one. **Long-lived integration trains are first-class queue entries** — a train is one entry tested as one merge commit, exactly as its merge is today. Stacked packet-trains pay the honest cost: each retargeted child enqueues sequentially (pipelined batteries — hours, not 4.7 minutes), which is precisely the protection the Stage-6 incident argues for.

**D6 — Rollout: two-step fast flip, with the flip's ordering and actor named.**

*Step 0 — pre-wire (near-no-op).* A short stacked train of by-file layers (deviation from the original "one PR" phrasing, so review stays tractable; the check-name churn on open PRs is the accepted cost): `merge_group` triggers on the D3 set; the **event-context audit** (every D3 member's use of `pull_request`-only payload fields gets a `merge_group` branch — `Stack fixture immutability`'s base ref, `jinn-agent CI`'s event gates, `Canonical docs check`'s explicit no-op, `hermetic-gate`'s SHA binding verified); uniquely-named terminal gate jobs; conversion of the four operator-surface workflows' `paths:` filters to job-level conditions; the **fast/full lane split** per D3's tiered-lanes ruling (fast mode on `pull_request`, diff-selected full battery on `merge_group`); and the required-set uniqueness + merge-group-handling + publish-scoping test. The only live behavior change is trigger plumbing that cannot fire until a queue exists — but it is "near"-no-op, not "pure": the gate-job renames change check names on every open PR, and reviewers should expect that. Step 0 lands **at least several days before the flip** so active PR heads accrue the new contexts through normal iteration.

*Step 1 — the flip.* Two motions by a named actor, back-to-back, and scripted: **(1)** the flip PR merges under the old rules — it carries the reworked `branch-protection-audit.mjs` (**re-pointed from the classic-protection API to the rulesets API** — a material rework, not a constant bump: ruleset vocabulary (`bypass_actors`, `dismiss_stale_reviews_on_push`), per-branch expectations split so `next` asserts the queue-era posture while `main` asserts its retained posture, and the dead `integration/evidence-v1` entry pruned), the new `enable-next-merge-queue.sh` mutation script (house precedent: the enable-*.sh pair, which it also retires/ports), and the hotfix runbook rewrite. **(2)** Immediately after that merge, a repo admin (Ritsu) runs the mutation script: split the Base ruleset per D2, create the `next` ruleset with required contexts + `merge_queue` rule per D5, add the `gh-readonly-queue/**` creation restriction (queue bot only — closes the forgeable-ref hazard GitHub patched in 2025-08), then `workflow_dispatch` the policy audit to confirm green. The window between (1) and (2) is minutes, and the audit only runs weekly or on dispatch, so no false red fires in the gap. **Open-PR migration:** PRs whose heads predate step 0 lack the new required contexts and cannot enqueue until their CI re-runs; the flip day includes a sweep nudging active PRs (re-run or author re-push), and Autopilot's merge stage stays paused from motion (2) until the D5 scripted verification passes, after which the enqueue-sweep (D4) takes over; humans enqueue via "Merge when ready" from the moment the queue exists. **Rollback is asymmetric by design:** drain the queue (stop enqueueing; let entries land or eject — the behavior of disabling with entries in flight is undocumented, so drain first), remove the `merge_queue` rule, keep the required-check layer — the gate layer is valuable with or without a queue — and dispatch the audit against the amended expectations. The four-phase alternative (required-checks-first with an isolated measurement window separating the gate layer's value from the queue's residual stale-green protection) was considered and rejected by operator ruling: speed to end-state preferred, compound-change attribution risk accepted. The stale-green measurement still accrues after the flip — post-merge reds of required contexts on `next` should fall to ≈0, and if they were already ≈0 the queue's residual value shows up as absence, which is accepted.

## Rationale

- **Native over external** because every external option costs a GitHub App with write access (a standing non-goal absent proof native fails), migration evidence in the wild runs one direction (toward native), and the single capability gap — flake tolerance — is a lever the house rule forbids using anyway. The design leans into that alignment: fix or quarantine the flake, never buy retry machinery.
- **The queue is only trustworthy if what it runs attests the merge result** — the brief's honesty core. D3's product-covering set is chosen against the actual failure evidence (Stage-6's red surfaces), not an abstract notion of "core checks"; the platform-only alternative would have blocked none of the tip's product reds. The same honesty cuts the other way: the v1 set's gaps (contracts, indexer, website, benchmark-product) are named as gaps, and two members' merge-group behavior (no-op, full-run) is stated rather than implied.
- **Exact-head becomes exact-landing.** The queue's tested commit is the commit that lands — verified empirically on a public queue-enabled repository (push-event counts per landed group; SHA identity between queue-branch head and base-branch commit; 2026-08-18), re-verified in this repo during the watch window rather than assumed. Rule 4's spirit — no unreviewed, untested code lands by agent action — is better served by "enqueue the exact reviewed head; land only what passed on the exact landing commit" than by the literal pre-queue text, which never guaranteed the landed result was tested at all.
- **Land-one-at-a-time** exists because the research falsified the assumed batching trade: batching saves no CI, and its only real effect (push granularity) breaks rule 8. Taking batching off the table lets the queue ship without touching the cadence rules — *conditionally*, per the unverified-successor mechanic D5 now carries openly.
- **Concurrency 2, timeout 180** because the binding constraint is the free-plan runner ceiling, not flake statistics: buying speculative depth costs idle-in-queue wall-clock that the timeout charges to entries, and ejection feedback loops are the failure mode to starve.
- **No direct pushes, no bypass actors on `next`** because the Stage-6 class of event *is* an untested push to `next`; a queue with a standing bypass hole protects against everything except the thing that motivated it. Dismiss-stale-on-push completes the same argument on the review axis, and was already the repo's codified intent.

## Alternatives considered

- **Mergify (OSS tier), Trunk Merge, Graphite, bors-ng.** Mergify adds batching-with-bisect, quarantine, pause/freeze, priorities — retained as the named escalation path (D1), not adopted: no evidence native fails here, and it costs a write-access App. Trunk's build-graph parallel lanes solve a FIFO-contention problem this repo does not have at 2–15 PRs/day. Graphite is the weakest on queue mechanics and strategically exposed post-acquisition. bors-ng is archived; its own maintainer points at GitHub native.
- **Single rollup queue-gate as the only required context.** Rejected for the size of the refactor it forces now (area CIs become reusable workflows) and the single point of configuration it concentrates — *not* for the vacuous-pass discipline, which D3's per-workflow gate jobs need exactly as much and solve the same way (`needs:` + `if: always()`, the pattern PAC already ships). The explicit set can migrate toward a rollup later without changing queue semantics.
- **Platform-gates-only required set.** Rejected: fails the honesty test against Stage-6 (see Rationale).
- **`HEADGREEN` grouping.** Rejected: buys flake tolerance by surrendering per-PR attribution — you no longer know which PR broke `next` — and substitutes a config toggle for the fix-or-quarantine rule.
- **Default batching (groups ≤5).** Rejected as primary: saves no CI, costs rule 8's per-PR canary. Retained as D5's named fallback if the land-one-at-a-time mechanic fails verification — engaging it amends rule 8 via its own docs PR.
- **Ancestor-plus-no-semantic-drift rule-4 rewrite with Autopilot as a direct merger.** Rejected: needs a queue bypass (contradicts D2's universality), and "no semantic drift" is unverifiable prose where the queue's CI-on-the-landing-commit is a mechanical check.
- **Agent PRs bypass the queue.** Rejected: reintroduces the untested-merge hole for the highest-volume PR class — the queue would protect `next` from exactly the PRs least likely to break it.
- **Species-bound review doctrine (human-only merge and review gates).** Retired by operator ruling (2026-08-18): reviews in this repo are agent-performed under operator credentials, so the human/agent line was prose, not mechanism. The enforced boundary is the credential set; DR-2026-06-03's CODEOWNERS mechanism survives as exactly that.
- **Four-phase staged rollout with an isolated measurement window.** Rejected by operator ruling in favor of the two-step flip (D6).
- **Retry-on-flake in the queue (blind re-enqueue loops).** Never on the table: standing house rule.

## What this DR enacts (and what remains)

**`docs/engineering/handbook.md` — rule 4, rewritten in full** (one physical line; replacement text is authoritative, shown wrapped for review):

```
4. **Reviewed, then queued.** (Rewritten by [DR-2026-08-18-b](../../log/decisions/2026-08-18-merge-queue-on-next.md), operator rulings 2026-08-18.) Review is a pipeline stage, not an identity ritual: every PR receives an agent review pass and carries an approving review from the **operator credential set** before it can enqueue; there is no separate human-review gate. The control surface is the credential set: CODEOWNERS paths require a code-owner credential (the CODEOWNERS + require-code-owner-review mechanism of DR-2026-06-03 is retained; its doctrine that an agent approval never satisfies the code-owner gate is superseded). **Which operator credential reviews is immaterial** — one operator's agent reviewing another operator's PR and the same operator's agent reviewing it are the same act; GitHub separately refuses to record an approval from the PR's own authoring account, so approvals land under a non-author operator credential as platform plumbing, not an independence claim. **Self-enqueue is permitted**: once review and required checks are green, any operator credential, including the author's, may enqueue (lineage: operator decision 2026-07-15, #1735) — the merge queue on `next` is the only merger of ordinary PRs. Autopilot pins enqueue to the exact reviewed head via the enqueue mutation's `expectedHeadOid`; the landed commit is the queue-constructed merge whose PR-side parent is that exact reviewed head and whose required checks passed on the commit that lands. Autopilot never supplies a missing approval, and never bypasses or weakens the queue or branch protection. Exceptions: (a) `fix(incident)` allows reviewer relaxation with documented justification; (b) **agent-authored mechanical conflict resolutions via the children ladder** (supersedes the merge-prep session from DR-2026-07-16 / #1756; Stage 5 of `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-21-single-surface-lifecycle.md`; `update-branch` ladder retired — the queue's speculative merge supersedes staleness handling) — for an approved, CI-green PR that is conflicted, Autopilot files `file-reconcile-child` work rather than claiming a merge-prep lease. Successful child resolution still leaves the new head subject to the full independent-review, CI, mergeability, and code-owner gates. Semantic conflicts, or any conflict touching a **code-owned path**, escalate to the operator (`Blocked on: Human`).
```
