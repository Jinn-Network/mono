# The Thesis — Narrative Version

## The real-world economy is a general intelligence

The global economy already functions as a general intelligence. Not metaphorically — functionally. You declare a desired state — a house built, goods shipped from Shenzhen, accounts audited — and a vast distributed system of autonomous actors achieves that state for you. Individuals, companies, supply chains, institutions. Nobody centrally coordinates the whole thing. It works through distributed actors following incentive structures, composing their capabilities, correcting errors through competition and feedback. That is what intelligence looks like at civilisational scale.

## The internet has no native equivalent of this

The internet can access the real-world economy. You can trigger real-world state restoration through it — order something online, hire someone through a platform, initiate a bank transfer. But every end-to-end execution path falls back into real-world infrastructure to actually execute: human cognition, legal systems, institutional trust, physical coordination. The internet itself has no productive capacity. It is a communication layer sitting on top of the real economy, not an economy of its own. There is no internet-native ability to transact end to end and achieve goals autonomously.

## Crypto and AI together make a native internet economy possible for the first time

Two preconditions have now converged.

Crypto provides the economic substrate: native value transfer, programmable agreements, permissionless participation — the equivalent of money, contracts, and property rights for the internet. AI provides computational cognition: systems that can do work autonomously, removing the dependence on human attention at every step in the execution path.

Neither alone is sufficient. Crypto without AI is financial plumbing with nothing flowing through it. AI without crypto is productive capacity trapped inside platforms with no native economic coordination layer.

Together, they enable end-to-end autonomous state restoration without falling back into the physical world's infrastructure. For the first time, the internet can have its own economy — not a digitisation of the existing one, but a native one.

## This economy will be larger in scale than the physical one

Every real-world transaction carries human maintenance costs: legal interpretation, cognitive judgment, trust verification, institutional overhead. When you strip human meatware out of end-to-end state restoration, these costs drop by orders of magnitude.

This has two consequences. First, the same outcomes become achievable with radically fewer resources. Second — and more importantly — activities that were previously non-viable because human coordination costs exceeded the value produced become viable. The frontier of possible economic activity expands massively. Not doing the same things faster, but making whole categories of activity sustainable that couldn't sustain themselves before.

### A ladder of scale

The mechanism is the same at every level: declare a desired state, the system achieves it. What changes is the number of people involved and the number of desired states held simultaneously.

**Individual, single desired state.** You want your online accounts secure — no unauthorised access across your digital presence. Currently you manage passwords, enable two-factor authentication, monitor breach notifications, rotate credentials, review access permissions. All manually, all requiring your ongoing cognitive overhead. You are the meatware maintaining this state. In the native internet economy, you declare the desired state — my accounts remain uncompromised — and an agentic system continuously monitors, rotates, revokes, and responds. The state is maintained without your attention. You set it and it holds.

**Group, single desired state.** An open-source project with fifty contributors wants its codebase to stay free of critical vulnerabilities. One shared desired state. Currently this requires maintainers volunteering attention — reviewing PRs, running security audits, triaging CVEs. The coordination cost is so high that most projects can't sustain it, so vulnerabilities accumulate. Declare the desired state — this codebase stays secure — and the system monitors dependencies, flags regressions, patches known issues, coordinates across contributors. Fifty people get the outcome without anyone having to be the unpaid maintainer.

**Individual, multiple desired states.** You run a small internet business. You want your site uptime above 99.9%, your customer data compliant with three jurisdictions' privacy laws, your content optimised for search visibility, your costs below a threshold, and your customer response time under two hours. Five desired states, all running simultaneously. Currently you juggle dashboards, hire contractors, read regulatory updates, manually intervene when things drift. Each desired state demands dedicated attention. In the native internet economy, you define all five. The system maintains each one continuously and independently. The cost of holding multiple desired states in parallel drops from "one human's full attention per state" to protocol-level overhead. You stop being the bottleneck in your own operation.

**Small group, multiple desired states.** A DAO of two hundred people pooling capital for investment. They share desired states around portfolio risk limits, transparency of reporting, voting participation thresholds, regulatory compliance across members' jurisdictions, and fair distribution of returns. Currently this requires fund administrators, legal counsel, auditors, compliance officers — institutional overhead that makes small-scale collective investment non-viable. The coordination cost exceeds the returns. Declare the desired states. The system enforces risk limits, generates reports, facilitates governance, handles compliance, distributes returns. Two hundred people get the coordination capacity of an institutional fund without the institution.

**Large group, multiple desired states.** Millions of participants in an internet-native economy. Each has their own map of desired states — data sovereignty preferences, service quality thresholds, resource allocation priorities, dispute resolution expectations, contribution-reward fairness standards. Currently the closest equivalent is platform governance — terms of service written by the platform, enforced by the platform, updated when the platform decides. Users get no input. The aggregation of millions of individual preferences into coherent collective outcomes is handled by nobody. In the native internet economy, each participant defines their desired states. The system aggregates, resolves conflicts, and maintains outcomes continuously. Not once every four years via a crude vote, but as a living, ongoing process across the entire participant base. The heavy bureaucratic machinery that currently fails to mediate between preferences and outcomes becomes unnecessary. The agentic layer is the governance.

## This economy should be built as open, credibly neutral infrastructure

This native internet economy can be built two ways: centralised (platforms own the coordination layer, extract from every transaction) or decentralised (open protocol, anyone can participate, infrastructure doesn't extract).

**Legitimacy.** A single credibly neutral system serving the world is a stronger coordination point than fragmented private companies. It attracts more capital, therefore more compute, therefore better capability. Two companies — however capable — are less legitimate and less of a focal point than a system that is plainly open and serves everyone equally.

**Extraction.** Currently, the major AI companies are not in maximum extraction mode. But the moment they are, it creates an overwhelming incentive to switch to something more neutral — the same dynamic that has driven every cycle of technological decentralisation. The systems that become durable infrastructure are the ones that don't extract: TCP/IP, Linux, HTTP. Every basis point of extraction above what's needed to sustain the system is rent, and rent is what triggers the next cycle of routing around.

**The backlash.** People are currently training their own replacements inside centralised systems. Developers using AI coding tools are feeding their work back into the models that will eventually do their jobs. This is no longer an abstract "my private data" concern — it is about livelihoods. When that becomes widely understood, the turn against centralised AI will be sharp. Open, decentralised systems inherit that flow. The more people using the open system, the more data it has, the faster it outpaces the centralised alternatives.

**Composability as structural requirement.** At every level of the scale ladder, each rung depends on capabilities from the rungs below. Small-group coordination uses the micro-services underneath. Governance uses all of it. A closed platform can optimise within its own boundary but cannot compose across boundaries it does not control. The combinatorial frontier of an open protocol is structurally larger than the linear frontier of a platform. The scale claim actually requires the architectural choice. You do not get the full expansion of viable activity without composability, and you do not get composability without openness.

Openness is not a moral preference layered on top of the economic argument. It is a structural prerequisite for the scale claim to hold.

## The binding constraint is the bridge

Abundant block space exists. Advancing AI capability exists. What is missing is the coordination layer — the connective tissue that lets crypto capital flow into AI compute and lets autonomous systems interact economically end to end. Targeted coordination of capital and compute around this specific problem.

That is the gap. That is where Jinn sits.
