## 1. The item

The evidence layer has no way to turn a private record into a publishable one. The engine that does
this exists — it is the scrub subsystem — but it is trapped inside `packages/core`, where nothing
in the new stack can reach it. Lifting it out is the largest remaining piece of the substrate.

This has the **Pipeline** role under `2026-07-25-evidence-layer-architecture.md`: it transforms a
record into another record and introduces no new record semantics.

## 2. What you are not designing

**You are not designing derivation semantics.** The protocol already specifies them, and they are
binding. Read these before anything else:

- **§10, Scrubbing and public representation** — the whole section. It defines derivation semantics
  explicitly and says so in its own first line: "This section defines derivation semantics, not a
  scrubber's detection rules, safety policy, or thresholds."
- **§6.8, Capture and derivation provenance** — what every scrubbed entity MUST carry.
- **§11.5** — a worked example of a scrubbed public representation, including the
  `provenance/scrub-receipt.json` artifact.

Your job is to design the **package that implements those semantics**, and to get the extraction
boundary right. Detection rules, safety policy, and thresholds are explicitly *not* protocol
concerns, which means they are yours to place.

## 3. Four constraints from protocol §10 that will shape the design

These are the ones most likely to be missed, and each has real structural consequence.

**Scrubbing creates new entities; it never substitutes.** A scrubbed derivative MUST NOT appear as
an `object` consumed by, or a `result` generated by, the Execution. It is a new entity linked to its
exact source with `prov:wasDerivedFrom` and to the transformation with the scrub activity. A design
that rewrites a record in place is wrong at the protocol level, not merely inelegant.

**A signed envelope is immutable.** A scrubber MUST NOT rewrite a DSSE payload, signature, or
subject digests in place. Unsafe material is scrubbed *before* signing and gets a new claim, or the
existing claim is withheld from publication entirely. So derivation and attestation have an
ordering relationship, and your design must state it.

**Derivation breaks the evaluation binding.** Protocol §10: "If a Result must change during public
derivation, an evaluation over the private Result does not transfer to the new bytes." Evaluations
bind to content digests, so scrubbing a Result orphans every evaluation over it. This is the
hardest consequence in the item. Your design must say what happens — whether the derivative is
publishable without its evaluations, whether re-evaluation over public bytes is required, or
whether such records are simply withheld.

**Entropy is not secrecy.** Scrubbers MUST understand protocol structure before redacting on
entropy alone. Digests, DSSE payloads and signatures, public keys, content identifiers, package
versions, and declared model identifiers are all expected high-entropy technical values. A
structure-blind detector applied to evidence records will destroy them.

## 5. The questions this design has to answer

**What is evidence-domain and what stays in `core`?** Not all 6,839 lines belong in the substrate.
Argue the boundary per group, not in aggregate.

**What is the port?** Presumably a raw record in, and a public derivative plus a receipt of what was
removed out — the receipt being the `provenance/scrub-receipt.json` from protocol §11.5. Define it
precisely, and ship a conformance kit with it if you are defining a port.

**Are the ML detectors in or out?** `gliner-detector.ts`, `transformers-detector.ts`, and
`ml-pii-stage.ts` bring heavyweight model dependencies. Under the layer rules, a heavyweight
dependency argues for a separate binding package. Decide, and say what a deployment without them
loses.

**Is derivation deterministic?** This matters more than it first appears. Derivatives are
content-addressed, so if an ML detector is non-deterministic across runs or model versions, two
derivations of the same input produce different digests. Determine whether the pipeline can promise
determinism, and if it cannot, say precisely what it promises instead and how the policy digest
recorded in the scrub activity (§10) pins it.

**Is the review queue substrate?** `review-queue.ts` is a human-in-the-loop mechanism. A shared
layer that blocks on a human is a strange shape. Decide whether it is substrate, application, or
something that should be split.

**What is the evaluation harness for?** `eval/` with its fixtures and bench runner measures detector
quality. Ask whether that is a shipped part of the package, a test-only surface, or a separate tool.

**Where does derivation sit in the producer's flow?** Before signing, per §10. Say what that means
for `execution-recorder` and `attestation-issuer`, and whether either needs to change. Do not change
them — say what would need to change and leave it as a follow-up.

## 6. Intersections to get right

- **`evidence-protocol`** — §6.8 and §10 are binding. Your package implements them; it does not
  reinterpret them.
- **`attestation-issuer`** — the scrub-before-sign ordering means these two have a sequencing
  relationship. The issuer already exposes a `DsseSigner` port (PR #2157); read
  `packages/attestation-issuer/src/prepare.ts` and `commit.ts` before asserting anything about it.
- **`publication`** — derivation runs before a public place in most producer flows, but the
  publication pipeline knows nothing about derivation and must stay that way. Confirm the
  composition root is where they meet.
- **`evidence-repository`** — both the private original and the public derivative are records with
  their own digests. Say whether they share a repository, and if not, what distinguishes them.
- **`packages/core`** — the extraction forces the question of what `core` is once scrub leaves.
  Note it; do not resolve it here.

## 7. Watch for

The size of this item makes two failure modes likely.

**Lifting the whole directory unexamined.** The point of the extraction is the boundary. A design
that moves 35 files and declares victory has done the easy half and skipped the one that matters.

**Letting the package become "the thing that handles secrets."** Apply the refusals discipline from
§2 of the shared context. Write the refusal table early and use it while you work, not after.
