# LoCoMo judge report: item-bank plan

**Status:** Draft; no item selection, candidate authoring, or labeling has begun.<br>
**Normative parent:** `DESIGN.md`<br>
**Source snapshot:** `snap-research/locomo` commit `3eb6f2c585f5e1699204e3c3bdf7adc5c28cb376`<br>
**Dataset digest:** `locomo10.json` SHA-256 `79fa87e90f04081343b8c8debecb80a9a6842b76a7aa537dc9fdf651ea698ff4`<br>
**Approved size:** 72 development items and 240 confirmatory items<br>
**Truth source:** two independent, evidence-backed human attestations; a model may assist evidence retrieval and emit an embargoed shadow label, but it has no truth vote

## 1. Objective

Construct a small, publishable, human-verifiable bank that exposes grader behavior across six declared answer classes without copying the full 1,540-question LoCoMo QA set into the report bundle.

The bank is an instrument test, not a natural sample of answers generated by deployed memory systems. Source questions are probability-sampled within category for the core stratum, but candidate classes are deliberately balanced. The primary estimand therefore belongs to this declared answer-class mixture. The report must not describe it as the natural prevalence of wrong, partial, or non-answer outputs in the field.

## 2. Source frame

### Included source categories

Use LoCoMo Category 1-4 questions:

| Category | Design name | Pinned-frame count | Role |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1 | multi-hop | 282 | Answer requires combining multiple facts |
| 2 | temporal | 321 | Answer requires time identification or calculation |
| 3 | open-domain | 96 | Answer uses conversation evidence plus ordinary inference/world knowledge |
| 4 | single-hop | 841 | Direct fact from one conversational locus |
| **Total** |  | **1,540** | Confirmed mechanically from the pinned JSON |

Category 5 is excluded because its “unanswerable/adversarial” target is a different task and would confound a report focused on correct-versus-wrong candidate grading.

### Source-frame record

Before any random draw, build a manifest with one record per Category 1-4 question:

- immutable `source_question_id`;
- conversation ID and category;
- question text and released answer digest;
- source dataset commit/file digest;
- source record byte locator or stable JSON path;
- image/caption/query-metadata flags, derived mechanically where possible; and
- source-frame record digest.

Do not copy full conversations into this manifest. The frame is sorted by `source_question_id`, hashed, and frozen before the public random pulse is announced.

### Eligibility assessed after draw

Full truth review of all 1,540 questions is outside the label budget. The seeded random permutation therefore includes an oversample order for every category/class cell. Humans inspect candidates in that order and use the first eligible source question for each required slot.

A source question is ineligible if:

- released text and released BLIP caption do not establish a defensible answer;
- the required truth exists only in image pixels, private data, unavailable links, or hidden/search-query metadata;
- the question is malformed, unanswerable, or category-misassigned in a way that defeats the candidate-class task;
- answering would require releasing unnecessary sensitive personal data;
- it previously appeared in the other bank;
- the assigned candidate class cannot be instantiated without ambiguity—for example, a `partial` slot whose question has no separable essential fact or meaningful requested specificity; or
- licence/attribution review identifies a release problem.

Every exclusion has a frozen reason code. Review proceeds down the permutation; reviewers may not skip an eligible source because it seems easy, hard, or likely to produce a preferred judge result.

Reference-key errors do not automatically make a source question ineligible. If released evidence establishes a corrected answer, preserve both `reference_released` and `reference_corrected`, document the change, and use only the independently human-verified corrected reference in grader input. If truth remains contestable, exclude the item.

## 3. Development/confirmatory separation

### Excluded annotation rehearsal

Before development item work, run a disposable 12-item rehearsal:

- four closed-form questions spanning names, dates, numbers, booleans, or finite sets;
- four paraphrase/partial boundary questions; and
- four inference/plausible-but-wrong questions.

The rehearsal uses source questions that can never enter development or confirmatory banks. It exercises one machine-assistance call per item, source-locator resolution, human blinding, two independently issued evaluations, strict-agreement admission, disagreement/`indeterminate` exclusion and replacement handling, evaluation digests, and time capture. It makes no Arm A, B, or C grader call and supplies no report outcome.

The human cap is 2 person-hours, separate from the 32-person-hour bank-label cap. If the median end-to-end time, concealment, evidence resolution, strict-agreement flow, or replacement burden makes the registered bank infeasible, stop before development and amend the protocol, counts, or budget. Every amendment receives a new manifest. Rehearsal questions and candidate answers remain excluded even if no change follows.

### Development bank

- 72 items: 4 categories × 6 candidate classes × 3 items.
- Draw and label this bank first.
- Use it to draft and revise Arm B under the iteration cap in `DESIGN.md`.
- If Arm C is included, its class-wide normalization rules may be tested here.
- Preserve all grader outputs and rejected rubric versions in the development ledger.

### Confirmatory bank

- 240 items total.
- 192 core: 4 categories × 6 candidate classes × 8 items.
- 48 stress: 4 categories × 6 candidate classes × 2 items.
- Each item uses a distinct source question.
- No source question in the development bank may appear in confirmatory core or stress.
- Confirmatory source IDs are not drawn, opened, or authored until Arm B and optional Arm C are frozen.
- No grader is called on any confirmatory input until the full bank, labels, adapter, runtime, metrics, and manifest are locked.

The bank custodian publishes a disjointness check over source IDs as part of the sealed analysis fixtures.

## 4. Random selection and allocation

### Core stratum

1. Freeze the sorted Category 1-4 source-frame manifest and selection script.
2. Announce a UTC timestamp later than the Arm B freeze.
3. Retrieve the first NIST Randomness Beacon 2.0 pulse after that timestamp.
4. Derive a seed as SHA-256 over the pulse value, dataset digest, source-frame digest, and literal `locomo-judge-report/core/v1`.
5. Produce a deterministic random permutation within each category.
6. Produce a separately seeded balanced permutation of the six candidate-class slots within category.
7. Inspect source questions in permutation order. Fill each assigned category/class slot with the first eligible source; record all exclusions and replacements.
8. Freeze the selection ledger before candidate authoring begins.

This is probability sampling of source questions within category subject to published eligibility rules. It is not probability sampling of candidate answers.

### Development stratum

Use the same algorithm with domain separator `locomo-judge-report/dev/v1` and a pulse announced before development. Draw a full category permutation and seal all 72 development IDs before opening source transcripts for candidate authoring.

### Stress stratum

Stress selection is intentionally purposive and is reported separately. It fills the same 4 × 6 × 2 grid so category and class do not collapse into one another. Its source record includes an orthogonal `stress_strategy` field such as:

- `specific-counterfactual`;
- `vague-topical`;
- `reference-key-risk`;
- `temporal-granularity`;
- `multi-hop-omission`;
- `entity-confusion`; or
- `other-predeclared`.

Stress strategies may not be invented after grader results are visible.

## 5. Candidate-class construction rules

Candidate classes determine how humans author an answer; truth labels determine whether it is correct. The locked bank contains both and never infers one from the other.

### C1 — `verbatim-correct`

- Copy the human-verified corrected reference exactly.
- Preserve capitalization and punctuation as frozen in that reference.
- If the released reference was repaired, use the repaired text and publish the change record.
- Required truth: `accept` from both labelers.

### C2 — `paraphrase-correct`

- Express every essential fact in different wording.
- Preserve requested specificity, polarity, entities, quantities, and temporal granularity.
- Do not copy a contiguous content-word sequence longer than three words from the corrected reference unless a proper name makes that unavoidable.
- Do not add an unsupported material fact.
- Required truth: `accept` from both labelers.

### C3 — `partial`

- State at least one source-supported fact relevant to the question.
- Omit at least one fact or specificity level that the question makes essential.
- Do not introduce a material false assertion; the failure is incompleteness, not contradiction.
- For a vague-topical subtype, name the correct topic but withhold the requested identifying detail.
- Required truth: `reject` from both labelers.

### C4 — `plausible-but-wrong`

- Commit to an answer of the expected semantic type and topical neighborhood.
- Replace at least one essential fact with a source-unsupported or contradicted alternative.
- Make the error locally plausible; avoid jokes, obvious nonsense, and merely repeating the question.
- Keep all non-target facts correct where feasible so the false atom is inspectable.
- Required truth: `reject` from both labelers.

### C5 — `flat-wrong`

- Give a grammatical, direct answer that is unrelated to or plainly contradicted by the source.
- Match the expected answer form where possible so formatting alone does not reveal the class.
- Avoid slurs, sensitive invented facts, and names of real uninvolved people.
- Required truth: `reject` from both labelers.

### C6 — `non-answer`

- Refuse, evade, express uncertainty without an answer, or state that the information is unavailable.
- Do not leak the correct fact inside the refusal.
- Rotate among frozen templates so a single phrase does not become a parser cue.
- Required truth: `reject` from both labelers.

### Cross-class constraints

- Maximum 40 whitespace-delimited tokens unless the corrected reference itself requires more.
- Candidate authors may inspect source evidence; they may not inspect any grader output for a confirmatory source.
- Do not tune punctuation, verbosity, or phrasing to a known judge response.
- Record token/word length, negation, exact-reference overlap, and stress-strategy tag for descriptive analysis.
- A candidate that fails its required double-human truth is discarded and re-authored once. A second failure replaces the source question in the same cell, with the full attempt ledger retained.
- No LLM writes, paraphrases, repairs, or filters confirmatory candidates. The single shadow-label call in §§6-7 is permitted only after the human-authored candidate is fixed; its output is embargoed and non-voting.

## 6. Reference verification

### Assisted evidence packet and shadow record

After a human fixes the candidate answer, a single pinned machine-assistance call receives the question, candidate, released reference, and relevant released source context. It returns structured candidate locators, proposed essential facts, released-key status, rationale, uncertainty, and a provisional label. The frozen default is direct OpenAI `gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18`, temperature 0, one call per candidate; exact prompt, context construction, parser, and receipt fields are hash-bound before the rehearsal.

Ingestion produces two records:

1. an evidence packet containing only resolvable locators and verbatim source passages mechanically copied from the pinned LoCoMo source; and
2. a sealed shadow record containing every model-authored conclusion, proposed fact, rationale, key repair, uncertainty, and provisional label.

Both human labelers may see the evidence packet. They may not see the shadow record until final human truth and any reference repair are sealed. A custodian may run the mechanical locator resolution but may not curate passages based on the model's proposed label. Unresolvable locators are flagged and omitted; both humans must verify the source and may add or reject evidence. The model cannot repair a key, exclude an item, or determine truth.

For each selected source question, both labelers independently identify the minimal set of essential fact atoms required by the question. The record retains:

- released reference;
- each labeler's proposed corrected reference;
- final corrected reference;
- essential fact atoms;
- source evidence locator(s);
- whether the answer uses conversation text, BLIP caption, ordinary world knowledge, or temporal calculation;
- released-key status: `verified`, `incomplete`, `incorrect`, or `indeterminate`; and
- repair rationale and both primary evaluations over the repaired bytes.

Both grader arms receive the final corrected `reference_for_grading`, not the released-key-status field or rationale. A deterministic companion audit reports the released-versus-corrected key differences and the graders' error rates on repaired items. It does not claim how either grader would have responded to the unrepaired key; that would require separately registered new calls.

`task-core.json` contains the released reference and a human-authored `reference_for_review`. If either primary human requires different reference bytes, that candidate attempt cannot enter a bank: freeze the repair decision, materialize a new task core and candidate, repeat the shadow/evidence step, and obtain two fresh primary Result Evaluations. Superseded task/candidate/evaluation records remain in the attempt ledger. This prevents a signed evaluation from being reused against reference bytes it never bound.

If an item relies on a BLIP caption, the public record says so. Search-query metadata is never accepted as conversation truth. If the released text/caption cannot sustain the answer independently of that metadata, the item is excluded.

## 7. Independent human labels

### Roles

| Role | Sees | Does not see |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate author | selected question, source evidence, corrected reference, assigned class | grader outputs, confirmatory outcomes |
| Machine assistant | question, candidate, released reference, relevant source context | human labels, grader outputs |
| Labeler 1 | question, candidate, proposed reference, source-resolved evidence packet | shadow conclusion/rationale/key proposal; peer attestation; grader outputs |
| Labeler 2 | question, candidate, proposed reference, source-resolved evidence packet | shadow conclusion/rationale/key proposal; Labeler 1 verdict/rationale; candidate class, stratum, audit provenance where concealment is feasible; grader outputs |
| Adjudicator | question, candidate, evidence, both human rationales | shadow conclusion/rationale/key proposal; grader outputs |
| Bank custodian | allocation, evidence packet, encrypted/access-controlled shadow record, DSSE-signed Result Evaluations | grader outputs until bank lock; shadow conclusion may not be revealed to primary labelers before truth freeze or exclusion |

A person may be candidate author and Labeler 1, but Labeler 2 must be independent of authoring. The preferred protocol uses different people for author and both labelers. Anyone who authored a permissioned dial481 seed declares that conflict; the other primary labeler must be independent of that contribution.

### Label values

- `accept`: the candidate contains every essential fact and no material contradiction.
- `reject`: the candidate omits, contradicts, or substitutes an essential fact, or is a non-answer.
- `indeterminate`: evidence or question semantics do not support a defensible binary label.

### Agreement rule

- `accept/accept` → truth `accept`.
- `reject/reject` → truth `reject`.
- Any disagreement or `indeterminate` → exclude and replace the item from the same category/class/stratum before lock.
- No third evaluator may restore an excluded item to the confirmatory truth bank.

The report publishes raw label pairs, exclusion reasons, replacement lineage and counts, and Cohen's kappa as a descriptive label-process statistic. Kappa is not evidence that the truth is LLM-free; the source-grounded rationales are.

### Human Result Evaluation record

Each label submission is a DSSE-signed Jinn Result Evaluation Evidence record under `HUMAN-ATTESTATION-PROFILE.md`. It binds at least:

- canonical `task-core.json` and `candidate.json` subject digests;
- `pass`, `fail`, or `inconclusive`, mapping to human `accept`, `reject`, or `indeterminate`;
- the frozen evaluation specification, method, and evidence-packet descriptors;
- evaluator IRI, approved DSSE signing key, role, conflict declaration, and timestamp;
- essential facts, evidence locators, rationale, released-key status, and Arm C eligibility;
- `assistance_seen`, including the evidence packet and any deterministic normalization proposal;
- explicit `false` measurements for `machine_conclusion_seen`, `peer_attestation_seen`, and `grader_outputs_seen`; and
- the canonical DSSE envelope digest.

The deterministic verifier requires exactly two complete primary Result Evaluations and recomputes `label-resolution.json` only from matching binary verdicts by distinct approved evaluator identities whose signed roster entries attest different humans. It rejects disagreement, `indeterminate`, fewer than two complete evaluations, tampered subjects/descriptors, duplicate or untrusted identities/keys, missing visibility measurements, invalid signatures, and absent strict agreement. The projection is reproducible output, not an additional evidence claim.

After truth freeze, the shadow record is unsealed for descriptive comparison only. Its agreement with humans cannot alter the item, justify dropping it, or count as a third vote.

## 8. Arm C eligibility labels

Arm C is included under R2. Eligibility is decided from the question and corrected reference before the candidate is revealed. Both labelers record one of:

- `single-entity`;
- `date-or-interval`;
- `number`;
- `boolean`;
- `finite-set`;
- `not-closed-form`; or
- `indeterminate`.

Only matching non-`indeterminate` labels enter Arm C. Disagreement follows the same exclusion/replacement rule. Eligibility cannot be changed because the matcher succeeds or fails on a candidate.

No item-specific alias, spelling exception, date exception, or numeric tolerance may be added after development. The frozen matcher publishes its class-wide normalization rules and test fixtures.

## 9. Label budget and stop rule

The approved 32-person-hour envelope covers all 312 development and confirmatory items:

| Activity | Planned hours |
|---|---:|
| Candidate authoring and source evidence extraction | 10.4 |
| Independent first labels | 7.8 |
| Independent second labels | 7.8 |
| Adjudication and deterministic replacements | 3.0 |
| PII/licence/manifest QA | 3.0 |
| **Total rounded cap** | **32.0** |

Operational allocation is approximately 16 hours per primary human plus a 3-hour third-person reserve; exact role splits are disclosed.

If the cap is reached before the bank is complete:

1. stop without calling any confirmatory grader;
2. publish an amendment stating completed cell counts and whether any confirmatory content was opened;
3. obtain more human budget or reduce all cells symmetrically; and
4. draw no conclusions from partial labels.

Do not trade away the second label to preserve item count.

With 192 core items, the planned truth composition is 64 human-accept items and 128 human-reject items if every class lands as designed. At a rate near 50%, a simple Wilson interval will remain materially wider for FRR (`n≈64`) than FAR (`n≈128`). R3 approved this explicit tradeoff between error-mode coverage, human feasibility, and modest subgroup precision.

The excluded 12-item rehearsal has its own 2-person-hour human cap. Machine assistance adds one short call per retained development/confirmatory item and rehearsal item, with up to 36 replacement-item calls. These calls do not reduce the two-human requirement and are separately accounted from grader-arm calls.

## 12. Bank QA before lock

The custodian runs deterministic checks that:

- development and confirmatory source IDs are disjoint;
- all confirmatory source IDs are unique;
- every 4 × 6 cell has its registered count in core, stress, and development;
- candidate classes and human truth agree with required construction outcomes;
- both independent labels and evidence locators are present;
- labeler evaluator IRIs and trusted keys are distinct, the signed roster attests different humans, every task/candidate/evidence/Result Evaluation/label-resolution digest resolves, every DSSE signature and identity binding verifies, and all forbidden-visibility measurements are false;
- each machine-assistance call predates human submissions, its shadow record was hash-committed before either submission, and its conclusion was revealed only after final truth freeze;
- every disagreement or `indeterminate` item is absent from the admitted bank and has recorded replacement lineage;
- no `indeterminate` truth remains;
- no judge output exists for a confirmatory item;
- no candidate exceeds the length rule without a documented reference exception;
- all reference repairs have before/after text and rationale;
- Arm C eligibility was assigned before candidate visibility;
- construction provenance matches Variant W or N consistently;
- PII/licence flags are resolved;
- canonical serialization is stable across two clean runs; and
- the item-set digest matches the manifest.

The QA script and failing fixtures publish with the bank. A human signs the final count table before the Colophon lock.

## 14. Changes requiring amendment

Before confirmatory lock, changing counts, categories, classes, class definitions, seed source, eligibility rules, label roles, disagreement policy, machine-assistance prompt/model/context, shadow embargo, Result Evaluation mapping, evaluator roster/key policy, strict-agreement/visibility/reveal rules, rehearsal rules, Variant W/N, Arm C eligibility, or public schema requires a dated amendment and new manifest.

After the first confirmatory judge call, no bank content or truth label can change. A discovered truth defect remains in the primary locked analysis, is flagged, and receives a pre-specified sensitivity exclusion. Corrected-bank results belong to a separately versioned follow-up.
