# Colophon — brand package

Decision-ready. Sections follow the brief's requested structure.

---

## 1. Executive recommendation

Build the product as a **publishing house for benchmark claims**, not another evaluation platform.

- **Name:** **Colophon** (KOL-uh-fon). Alternates held: **Vernier**, **Bakeoff**.
- **Category descriptor:** *benchmark publishing for agent configurations*.
- **Five-second read:** *Compare agents on the same work.*
- **Deeper promise:** *Publish benchmark claims people can check.*
- **Territory:** The Press — a claim is a publication, and every publication states how it was made.
- **Flagship artifact:** the public report. Everything else in the system is an entrance to it.
- **Jinn:** factual attribution only, two approved strings ("Built on Jinn." as the imprint form;
  "Built on Jinn, by Jinn contributors." where the relationship is described in prose), in the report
  imprint, the infrastructure/about page, docs and verification pages. Nowhere else.

Why this and not the alternatives: the eval category already owns "dashboard" and "observability."
Nobody owns "the artifact a skeptic reads." The word *colophon* names precisely the thing the product
adds to a number — the statement of how it was made — and it does so without borrowing certification
language the product cannot back.

## 2. Product and audience interpretation

**Customer:** open-source coding-agent, harness, skill, plugin, tool and loadout builders about to
make a public performance claim. **Operating user:** frequently a delegated agent. **Reader:** a
skeptic — a maintainer, a procurement lead, a researcher, or another agent.

Three parties, three needs:

| Party | Needs | Consequence for the brand |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsor (human) | A result they can put in a release without being embarrassed later | The brand must look like it survives scrutiny, not like it sells confidence |
| Operator (agent) | Stable verbs, explicit states, machine-readable artifacts | Vocabulary is a design surface; names must survive an audit log |
| Reader (human or agent) | To check the claim cheaply | The report is the product's face, not a dashboard export |

**The reputational risk being reduced:** publishing a number that gets taken apart in public. The
2025–26 pattern the customer has watched repeatedly is a vendor-run benchmark that reproduces lower,
followed by a correction thread. Colophon's job is to make the claim survive that thread — by having
already published the method, the accounting, the disagreement and the failures.

**What the customer gains beyond a score:** a distribution asset. A report that is worth linking,
a badge that is worth putting in a README, and a method other people can clone — which turns a
one-off result into a small standard other builders measure against.

4. Nobody in the category looks like print. Everything looks like a console.

## 4. Brand opportunity

> The category sells confidence in private. Colophon sells a claim that survives in public.

Three ownable moves:
1. **Print, not console.** Warm paper, hairline rules, footnotes, an imprint. Instantly non-generic
   in a screenshot next to any competitor.
2. **Six verdicts, not two.** A palette and texture system that can render "we don't know" as
   legibly as "passed." No other eval brand can draw doubt.
3. **The imprint.** A recurring block that states how the work was made — the namesake, and the
   thing that makes a Colophon result recognizable when it is embedded in someone else's README.

## 5. Positioning and message hierarchy

**Positioning statement**
For open-source agent, harness, skill and loadout builders who are about to make a public
performance claim, **Colophon** is the place to run the comparison and publish the result — because
a Colophon report shows its method, its accounting and its failures, so the claim survives inspection.
Unlike evaluation platforms built for internal quality assurance, Colophon's output is a public
artifact designed to be checked.

**Message hierarchy**
1. **Compare agents on the same work.** (category, 5 seconds)
2. **Publish benchmark claims people can check.** (promise, 15 seconds)
3. Three reasons to believe (60 seconds):
   - *The method is fixed before the official result exists* — sealed with a digest and a timestamp.
   - *You choose the assurance, and the report says which one you chose* — from deterministic tests
     to multiple evaluators with disagreement retained.
   - *Every expected execution is accounted for* — failures, cancellations and missing results stay
     in the denominator and on the page.
4. Supporting (deeper): agent-native operation; two or more configurations, not just A/B; portable
   evidence; clone, rerun and challenge; competitive execution pricing.

**Taglines**
1. *Compare agents on the same work.* (primary; category-first)
2. *Claims that show their method.* (brand line)
3. *Publish a number people can check.* (campaign)
4. *Same work. Several agents. One fixed method.* (technical audience)
5. *Every run accounted for.* (accounting/rigor)

**Short description (≤ 40 words)**
Colophon runs two or more agent configurations against the same tasks, records how every execution
turned out, and publishes a report whose method, evidence and failures anyone can inspect, cite,
clone or rerun.

**Long description**
Colophon is where builders take a performance claim public. You import or select a task set,
configure two or more entrants, and decide what counts as success and how a delivery becomes a
verdict — deterministic tests, a single evaluator, an evaluator separated from the solver, or several
evaluators with majority or unanimous agreement and disagreement retained rather than resolved. You
preview, get a quote, and lock the method; the lock is sealed with a content digest and a timestamp
before any official execution runs. Colophon then executes across network operators or your own
runner, keeps a full accounting of every expected execution including the ones that fail or never
return, and assembles a public report: the compared configurations, the fixed method, the evaluation
policy and evaluator agreement, cost and runtime, visible failures, stated limitations, and a
machine-readable claim. Readers can cite it, embed it, clone the benchmark, rerun it, or challenge
the result. Authorized agents can perform the entire workflow. Colophon records how a result was
produced; it does not certify that a result is correct.

**Landing hero**
> **Compare agents on the same work.**
> Run two or more configurations against one task set, choose how the results are judged, and publish
> a claim whose method, evidence and failures anyone can inspect.
> [Start a benchmark] [Read a published report]

**Report headline pattern**
`<What was compared> on <task set> (<n> tasks)` — e.g. *Three harness loadouts on the same 500 tasks*.
Subhead states the finding and its cost, then the caveat: *"88 tasks drew a split verdict and are
published as conflicted."* Never a superlative, never "best."

**Social-card headline pattern**
`<Claim value> · <n> tasks — <suite>`, plus the execution status field (Observed / Attested /
Conflicted / Incomplete) and the entrant ranking. The card can never state something the report does
not; if the run is conflicted or incomplete, the card says so.

**README badge language**
`colophon | 71.1% · 500 tasks | Observed` — three fields: source, value with denominator, and how it
was obtained. Not "verified," not a checkmark, always a link to the full report.

## 6. Brand territories

### Territory A — **The Press** *(recommended)*

- **Central idea:** a benchmark claim is a publication, and every publication carries a colophon
  stating how it was made.
- **Promise:** your result travels with its method attached.
- **Reason to believe:** method digest and lock timestamp; full accounting; published disagreement;
  recomputable claim JSON; clone and rerun.
- **Tone:** editorial, exact, unhurried. A serious technical periodical, not a lab and not a court.
- **Naming logic:** printing and publishing vocabulary — Colophon, Imprint, Folio, Errata, Recto.
- **Verbal style:** sentence case, denominators always present, limitations in body type. Second
  person for the builder; third person for the artifact.
- **Visual principles:** warm paper and warm ink; hairline rules instead of shadows; a real
  typographic mark (asterism); footnotes and marginalia; radii 0–4px; one accent ink.
- **Strengths:** unmistakable beside competitors; the report becomes the brand; naturally honest
  about uncertainty (print has always had errata, footnotes and "n = ").
- **Weaknesses:** print metaphors can read old-fashioned; needs discipline to stay legible in a CLI
  and a dense app shell; serif type must be handled well or it looks like a blog.
- **Category confusion risk:** could be mistaken for a publishing tool or a documentation product —
  mitigated by keeping the category descriptor visible on every entry surface.
- **Trust risk:** an imprint block can start to *feel* like a seal. Mitigation: the imprint is a
  factual list, never a stamp; no ribbons, no crests.
- **Difference from Jinn:** no sigils, no mythology, no protocol language; a common English noun from
  publishing, warm paper palette, editorial serif — a different world entirely.
- **Examples:** landing hero above; report as in `ui_kits/report`; social card as `ReportCard`.

### Territory B — **The Instrument**

- **Central idea:** the product is a measuring instrument. It does not judge; it measures, and states
  its own tolerance.
- **Promise:** the same work, measured the same way, every time.
- **Reason to believe:** fixed method; repeat runs; variance and confidence shown by default; tolerance
  stated with every figure.
- **Tone:** cool, exact, engineering-grade. Calipers, not gavels.
- **Naming logic:** metrology — Vernier, Caliper, Gauge, Micron, Datum.
- **Verbal style:** terse, numeric, unit-obsessed. "±" appears everywhere.
- **Visual principles:** graduated scales, tick marks, precise thin grids, one cool accent, technical
  drawing conventions; monochrome plus a single measurement colour.
- **Strengths:** highest perceived rigor; excellent for data-visualization character; distinct from
  observability dashboards.
- **Weaknesses:** cold; makes the marketable-claim job harder — instruments don't sell stories; risks
  implying an accuracy the product cannot promise about evaluator judgement.
- **Category confusion risk:** reads like a profiling/benchmarking *performance* tool (latency, cost),
  not a claim publisher.
- **Trust risk:** "instrument" implies objectivity. The product's hardest boundary is precisely that
  it is *not* objective about verdicts.
- **Difference from Jinn:** metrology vocabulary, cool neutral palette, drafting aesthetics.
- **Name status:** **Vernier collides** with Vernier Science Education (long-standing live software
  and hardware marks in education/measurement). Would need a different name inside the territory.

### Territory C — **The Open Contest**

- **Central idea:** rules posted before the start, several entrants, one course, results in public.
- **Promise:** a fair, watchable comparison anyone can enter or rerun.
- **Reason to believe:** the entry rules and the course are published before the run; anyone can
  clone the course and enter their own configuration; challenges are a first-class action.
- **Tone:** energetic, sporting, communal, plain-spoken.
- **Naming logic:** contests and trials — Bakeoff, Heat, Fieldday, Openrun, Startline.
- **Verbal style:** short, active, human. "Enter", "run", "post your result".
- **Visual principles:** lane charts, start lines, entry cards, bold numerals, a livelier palette,
  a scoreboard rhythm.
- **Strengths:** the most memorable and the most shareable; leans naturally into the distribution
  goal; makes the multi-entrant (not A/B) story obvious.
- **Weaknesses:** gravity deficit — hard to use in procurement; invites exactly the leaderboard
  reading the brief warns against ("who is objectively best?").
- **Category confusion risk:** reads as a public leaderboard or a hackathon, not a publishing tool.
- **Trust risk:** highest. Contests imply winners; the product must not crown anyone.
- **Difference from Jinn:** sporting vernacular, bright palette, no mythology.

**Not proposed:** three variants of minimal scientific software. A, B and C differ in what the product
*is* — a press, an instrument, a contest — not in palette.

clearance.

## 9. Verbal identity

**Tone-of-voice principles**
1. **State, don't sell.** Facts with numbers beat adjectives. "1,408 of 1,500 executions returned"
   does more than "comprehensive coverage."
2. **Carry the denominator.** Every figure appears with what it is out of and when it was measured.
3. **Limitations in body type.** Caveats are set at the same size as results, never as grey fine print.
4. **Name the boundary.** Where a guarantee stops, say where it stops in the same sentence.
5. **No superlatives, no winners.** The report describes a comparison; the reader draws the conclusion.
6. **One voice for humans and agents.** The verb in the button is the verb in the API.

**Person and casing:** second person for the builder ("you lock the method"); third person for the
artifact ("this report records…"). Sentence case throughout, except small-caps labels. No emoji.
No exclamation marks.

**Prefer:** method locked before execution · expected executions · returned · accounted for ·
observed by Colophon · self-reported · evaluator-attested · conflicted · retained disagreement ·
incomplete · unverifiable · recomputable · inspectable · clone · rerun · challenge · scoped to
the full report.

**Avoid, or use only under the policy below:** verified · certified · proven · independent · trusted ·
official · audited · guaranteed · best · state-of-the-art · objective · ground truth · seal ·
accredited · validated.

**Vocabulary policy for the loaded words**

| Word | Permitted use | Never |
|---|---|---|
| verified | Only of a *recomputation*: "this report's claim JSON recomputes from the published evidence." | As a status badge, as a synonym for good, as a bare stamp |
| independent | Only as *solver-separated*: "the evaluator identity is not the solver identity." | To imply unrelated real-world parties |
| audited | Not used. | Anywhere |
| certified / accredited | Not used. | Anywhere |
| proven | Not used of results. | Anywhere in product or campaign copy |
| official | Only of the run: "the official run" = the executions counted under the locked method. | Of the product, of Colophon, of a verdict |
| trusted | Not used. | Anywhere |
| observed | Colophon executed and recorded it. | Of self-reported local runs |
| attested | A named identity asserts it; Colophon records the assertion. | As proof |

**Honest framing of verification and independence — the three sentences that must always be available**
1. "Colophon observed these executions and recorded their outputs. That establishes what was run and
   what came back."
2. "It does not establish that the evaluators reached correct verdicts."
3. "Multiple evaluator identities are not multiple unrelated parties. Separation is a property of the
   configuration, not of the world."

**Agent-facing action vocabulary (stable, explicit, versioned)**
`import_tasks` · `configure_entrant` · `set_assurance` · `preview_run` · `request_quote` ·
`lock_method` · `launch_run` · `cancel_run` · `inspect_result` · `draft_report` ·
`publish_report` · `cite_report` · `clone_benchmark` · `rerun_benchmark` · `challenge_report`

Rules: no synonyms, no cute verbs, no renaming without a version bump. The same string appears in the
button, the permission dialog, the audit log and the API. States are explicit and finite:
`draft · previewed · quoted · locked · running · cancelled · complete · published · amended · challenged`.

**"Built on Jinn." attribution copy** — see §14.

## 10. Visual identity directions

### A — The Press *(developed in §11)*
Wordmark in an editorial serif; asterism mark; warm paper/ink; hairline rules; footnotes; bar-and-rule
data visualization; texture-encoded evidence; near-zero radii; motion that ticks rather than glows.

### B — The Instrument
Wordmark in a grotesque with engraved detailing; symbol is a vernier scale fragment (two rules
offset). Type: Söhne / Roboto Mono. Colour: graphite and bone with a single cyan measurement accent.
Layout on a fine 4px technical grid with visible tick rules. Data-visualization is scales and
whiskers; uncertainty shown as tolerance bands. Icons are 1px engineering line. Motion is
step-wise, snapping to graduations. Dark mode is the primary mode. Accessibility: excellent contrast,
but heavy reliance on thin rules is a risk at low resolution.

### C — The Open Contest
Wordmark bold and horizontal; symbol is a start line (three stacked lanes). Type: a condensed grotesque
with big numerals. Colour: chalk white, lane colours per entrant, one hot signal colour. Layout is
scoreboard-like, wide and airy. Data-visualization is lane charts and race bars; uncertainty appears
as an unfinished lane. Icons are chunky filled. Motion advances in lanes. Dark mode is stadium-dark.
Accessibility: lane colour is the primary channel, so it needs a redundant pattern to stay usable —
the same problem Colophon solves with texture.

## 11. Recommended identity system — The Press

**Mark.** An **asterism** (⁂) redrawn as three ink diamonds. It is a real typographic mark used to
divide a text, and it reads as several entrants set against one comparison. It is not a shield, a
seal, a checkmark, an orb or a mesh. Files: `assets/mark.svg`, `mark-open.svg`, `mark-rule.svg`,
`logo-lockup.svg`, `favicon.svg`.

**Wordmark.** "Colophon" set in Newsreader regular, tracking −0.025em. Lockup places the mark at cap
height to the left with clear space equal to the cap height of the C. Optional descriptor sits to the
right of a hairline rule in small caps.

**Typography.** Newsreader (display + reading body) · Public Sans (UI, labels) · IBM Plex Mono (IDs,
data, CLI, machine artifacts). 15px UI base, 17px reading body, 1.26 scale. Mono is not decoration:
if a value is machine-generated, it is set in mono.

**Colour.** Paper `#f7f4ed`, ink `#14120e`, one accent — printer's vermilion `#c7402a` — for the
mark, live state and rules of emphasis, **never** for "good." Indigo carries links and attestation;
moss and ochre carry met and conflicted. Dark theme inverts to warm black.

**Evidence and uncertainty.** Six verdicts (met, unmet, conflicted, attested, incomplete,
unverifiable) each with a colour and a fill texture: solid = observed, 45° hatch = attested,
vertical rule = conflicted, dot screen = missing. Texture is redundant with colour, so the state
survives greyscale printing and colour-blind reading.

**Data-visualization character.** Bars and rules, never gradients or glows. Accounting bars are
denominated by *expected* executions so missing work occupies real width. Ranking bars use vermilion
for the reference entrant and neutral ink for the rest — position and label carry the ranking, not hue.

**Layout.** Three widths: 1240px app shell, 880px report, 720px prose. Sections open with a heavy
2px rule and a mono section number in the margin. Footnotes are a first-class element.

**Iconography.** Lucide, 1.6px stroke, 15–16px in UI. Never a checkmark used as a verdict, never a
shield, never a badge-with-ribbon. See readme.md § Iconography.

**Motion.** `cubic-bezier(.2,0,0,1)`, 120–260ms. Live runs *tick* on a one-second cadence — counters
increment, bars extend. Nothing pulses, glows, bounces or shimmers. `prefers-reduced-motion` zeroes
all durations.

**Light and dark.** Light is the default and the canonical report mode (reports print). Dark is warm
black, used for the agent-native marketing block and for the app at the user's preference — never for
the published report by default.

**Applied surfaces** (built in this system):
landing page · application shell · configuration comparison table · assurance selector · live
benchmark state · public report · evaluator disagreement · completeness accounting · social card ·
README badge · report embed · CLI output · API docs · claim JSON · "Built on Jinn." imprint line.

## 12. Public-report and claim-asset system

**Hierarchy.** Report (complete) → Report card / OG image → Badge → Claim JSON. Everything below the
report is a *pointer*, and every pointer resolves to the report. A badge that does not resolve is not
permitted to be generated.

**Report card (`ReportCard`).** 600×~340 design, exported at 1200×630. Fields: headline, suite and
task count, date, ranked entrants with bars, and an execution status line. Status is one of
*Observed by Colophon · Self-reported execution · Contains conflicted results · Incomplete accounting*.
If a run is conflicted or incomplete, the card must say so — the status field cannot be suppressed.

**Badge (`ClaimBadge`).** Three fields: `colophon` | value with denominator | status. Status values:
Observed · Attested · Conflicted · Incomplete · Draft. There is deliberately **no "Verified" badge and
no green tick**. Colour follows the verdict palette, and the status word carries the meaning so the
badge works in monochrome.

**Verification status wording.**
- *Observed* — Colophon executed and recorded these runs.
- *Attested* — a named identity ran them and asserts the outputs; Colophon recorded the assertion.
- *Conflicted* — evaluator identities disagreed on some tasks and the split was retained.
- *Incomplete* — some expected executions did not return; they remain in the denominator.
- *Draft* — not published; the method may still change.

**Local versus network execution.** Network execution earns *Observed*. Local runner execution earns
*Attested* and is drawn with the 45° hatch everywhere it appears — in the table, in the bar, on the
card and on the badge. The distinction is never implied by absence; it is always stated.

**Incomplete and conflicted runs.** Both are rendered, never hidden behind a toggle default. The
accounting bar reserves their width. The report's §03 states their counts in body type. A report with
more than a stated threshold of unreturned executions carries a limitation callout automatically.

**Embed.** `<iframe src="…/embed">` renders the report card plus a one-line accounting summary and a
"Full method and accounting in the report" link. The embed never renders a score alone.

**Claim JSON.** Fields include `claim`, `method_digest`, `method_locked_at`, `expected_executions`,
`returned`, `conflicted`, `execution` (`network-observed` | `local-attested`), and `report` URL.
An agent reading only this object gets the accounting, not just the number.

## 13. Agent-native brand considerations

1. **Vocabulary is identity.** The action list in §9 is a brand asset with the same status as the
   logo. It is versioned, and changes require a version bump.
2. **States are finite and named.** Ten states, one word each, identical in UI and API.
3. **Every visual state has a machine equivalent.** Six verdicts, four textures, five badge statuses —
   each maps 1:1 to a string in the claim schema.
4. **Agents are shown as operators, not as subjects.** The app's agent activity log uses the same
   verbs a human sees, at the same type size, in the same layout.
5. **Approval is a designed moment, not a modal afterthought.** Spend, lock, cancel and publish render
   as explicit human-approval steps with the consequence stated in body type.
6. **Nothing in the identity requires colour to be understood.** Texture and words carry every state,
   so a text-mode agent reading the CLI or the JSON gets the same information a reader gets.

## 14. Jinn attribution policy

**Approved strings:** two, by context. "Built on Jinn." is the imprint form, used wherever the
attribution stands alone as a bare line. "Built on Jinn, by Jinn contributors." is the relationship
form, used only where the relationship is described in prose — the infrastructure / about page, per
the approved wording below. Nothing else. No logo lockup, no co-branding, no colour, type or motif
borrowed, no "powered by" variants, no Jinn terminology in workflow language.

**Appears in:**
- the report imprint, as the last, smallest line;
- the site footer legal line;
- the infrastructure / about page, where the relationship is described in one paragraph;
- technical verification pages and docs, where execution provenance is explained;
- the claim JSON, as an `infrastructure` field.

**Never appears in:** product name · hero · primary navigation · workflow terminology · report
headlines · social cards · badges · campaign language · the visual identity.

**Prominence:** smallest type on the page, `--text-faint`, no mark, no link styling stronger than
body links. It should read as a manufacturing fact, like a printer's imprint at the back of a book.

**Infrastructure page wording (approved; revised 2026-08-11 by operator ruling —
see below):**
> Colophon runs benchmark executions on Jinn infrastructure. Jinn provides the execution, evidence
> and verification layers; Colophon defines the benchmark method, the evaluation policy, the
> accounting and the published report. Built on Jinn, by Jinn contributors.

**Why this wording changed.** The original read "Jinn provides the execution and *settlement* layer"
and closed "Colophon is an *independent product* and is not the canonical interface to Jinn." Both
clauses were struck:

- *Settlement* names a capability the product does not ship. Nothing settles on the self-run venue,
  and the word invites a reader to infer an economic guarantee that does not exist.
- *Independent product* asserts organizational independence Colophon does not have — it is built by
  the same contributors, in the same repository. That is precisely the class of claim the product
  design's must-not-imply rules forbid, and this document's own word policy already restricts
  "independent" to solver-separation and bars it from implying unrelated real-world parties. A
  skeptical reader who traces the attribution line to the Jinn repository — one click — would find a
  product about verifiable claims making an unverifiable one about itself.

"Built on Jinn, by Jinn contributors." states the relationship without characterizing it, and needs
no separate disclaimer about canonical interfaces: the design's tier rules already establish that
Colophon is not Jinn's frontend, and the sentence claims nothing that a reader could catch us
overstating.

**When to omit:** any surface where the reader's question is "what is this product and what does it
do" — hero, pricing, onboarding, the app shell, social assets.

## 15. Accessibility and international considerations

- **Contrast:** body and UI text meet WCAG AA on paper and on warm black. Verdict chips place the
  verdict colour on its own tinted background, tested to AA for the text pairing; the texture overlay
  is capped at 0.28 opacity so it never depresses contrast below AA.
- **No colour-only meaning.** Every verdict is colour + texture + word. Greyscale printing of a report
  loses nothing.
- **Motion.** `prefers-reduced-motion` sets all durations to 0. The live-run tick is a value change,
  not an animation, so it degrades cleanly.
- **Focus.** 2px indigo focus ring at 2px offset on every interactive element; ring colour is distinct
  from every verdict colour.
- **Type size floors.** 11px only for uppercase small-caps labels with 0.08em tracking; 12px minimum
  for sentence-case UI text; 17px reading body.
- **Screen readers.** IconButton requires a label. Verdict chips read their word, not their colour.
  Tables use real `<th>` scope.
- **Language.** "Colophon" is a Greek-derived word present in several European languages; it is
  unfamiliar in CJK markets and should always appear with the descriptor on first use. The descriptor
  translates cleanly; the taglines rely on "same work," which translates well. Numerals and dates are
  ISO throughout to avoid locale ambiguity. Right-to-left: layout is rule-and-column based with no
  directional metaphors, so mirroring is mechanical.
- **Fonts:** all three faces are open-licence with broad Latin coverage; CJK and Cyrillic require a
  documented fallback stack before those markets.

## 16. Risks and unresolved decisions

1. **Trademark.** "Colophon" is a common noun; the mark will be weak in isolation. File the wordmark
   plus the asterism device together. **Professional clearance not yet done.**
2. **Domain.** `colophon.com` almost certainly unavailable. `colophon.press` is on-metaphor and
   preferred; not yet acquired. Report URL structure depends on this decision.
3. **Comprehension.** Does "Colophon" clear the five-second test with the descriptor attached? Test 9
   in §17 is the gate. If it fails, Folio (in-territory) or Bakeoff (out-of-territory) is next.
4. **Serif at small sizes.** Newsreader is used for reading body; verify at 15px on Windows/ClearType
   before committing.
5. **Badge misuse.** Someone will screenshot a badge and drop the link. Mitigation options not yet
   decided: signed badge images, a short claim ID rendered inside the badge, or a public
   badge-resolution endpoint.
6. **"Attested" comprehension.** Untested. If readers hear "attested" as "verified," the local-run
   distinction collapses. Fallback wording: "self-reported."
7. **Threshold for the automatic incompleteness callout.** Not set. Needs a product decision.
8. **Font licensing.** No binaries were supplied; the system currently loads open faces from Google
   Fonts. If a licensed pairing is intended, it must replace these before launch.
9. **Report versioning and errata.** The imprint supports "amended," but the amendment UX, the
   notification policy and whether old versions stay addressable are undecided.
10. **Scope beyond coding agents.** The name stretches; the default task-set vocabulary ("tasks,"
    "suites," "deliveries") should be checked against a non-coding domain before it hardens.

## 17. Recommended validation tests

| # | Test | Audience | Method | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Five-second category | 25 OSS agent builders | Show hero for 5s, ask "what does this do?" | ≥70% say some form of "compare agents / benchmark them" |
| 2 | Credibility | 15 evaluation engineers | Read one report, rate "would you trust this in a release?" and say why | Method lock and accounting named unprompted by ≥half |
| 3 | Certification boundary | 20 mixed readers | After reading a report, ask "did Colophon certify this result?" | ≥80% say no; ≥half can explain what it *did* establish |
| 4 | Memorability | 30 builders | 48h delayed recall of name + category | ≥50% recall the name; ≥70% recall the category |
| 5 | Distinction from Jinn | 20 people familiar with Jinn | Show landing, app and report; ask who makes this and what Jinn's role is | ≥80% describe Jinn as infrastructure, not the product |
| 6 | Distinction from eval platforms | 20 builders | Show Colophon plus three competitor screenshots unlabelled; ask which is which and how they differ | Colophon identified as the odd one out; "public report" named as the difference |
| 7 | Share intent | 12 maintainers about to ship a release | Give a real report of their own work; ask if they'd put the badge in the README | ≥6 actually do it |
| 8 | Agent terminology | 8 teams building agent integrations | Give the action list without docs; ask them to predict each verb's effect and permission scope | ≥90% correct prediction; zero collisions in meaning |
| 9 | Name gate | 40 builders | Name + descriptor, five seconds, then recall and spelling | ≥70% comprehension, ≥60% correct spelling |
| 10 | Badge honesty | 20 readers | Show conflicted and incomplete badges; ask what each means | Nobody reads either as a pass |
| 11 | Machine legibility | Agent harnesses | Give only `claim.json`; ask the agent to summarize the claim and its limits | Limits reported, not just the score |
| 12 | Greyscale / colour-blind | 10 readers incl. deuteranopia | Print a report in greyscale; identify each verdict | 100% identification via texture and word |
