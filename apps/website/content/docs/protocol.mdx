The Jinn protocol is written only in [Jinn-Network/spec](https://github.com/Jinn-Network/spec). That repository holds the normative text, the machine-readable schemas, the conformance vectors, the changelog, and the process by which any of it changes. It holds no implementation. Its first release, [v0.1.0](https://github.com/Jinn-Network/spec/releases/tag/v0.1.0), was published on 24 September 2026.

## What it says

The protocol says how work is described, done, evidenced, judged, trusted and found. It takes no fee and no part in payment.

It sets a floor: [any two implementations](https://github.com/Jinn-Network/spec#what-two-implementations-must-be-able-to-do), written from the text alone, must be able to read each other's records, agree on what they say and whether they are valid, and find each other's records.

## Records

Every record is a sealed JSON document with a published schema, a stable identifier and a digest. One record refers to another by its digest, and where a record is stored is never its identity. The specification defines [the record families](https://github.com/Jinn-Network/spec#the-record-families). A record kind that an application defines on top of the protocol, such as Colophon's sealed benchmark claims, follows the same disciplines, and its owner publishes it.

No Jinn code is needed to check a record. The rules are in [Verifying a record by hand](https://github.com/Jinn-Network/spec#verifying-a-record-by-hand).

## Offers

The holder of a record can publish an offer for it. An offer names the record by its digest, gives its price, or no price for a free offer, and names the gate where a buyer asks for it. The protocol carries offers and lets anyone find and verify them. It does not settle payment: payment for a priced record goes straight to the holder, outside the protocol.

Offers exist in the protocol today. Buying and selling records is the direction, not a working market. The terms are defined in the [glossary](https://github.com/Jinn-Network/mono/blob/next/GLOSSARY.md#records-and-offers), which points at the specification as the authority.

## Build on it

- **Read the text and the schemas.** They are under [`documents/`](https://github.com/Jinn-Network/spec/tree/main/documents), at the paths their identifiers resolve to.
- **Run the conformance vectors.** Every family ships [vectors](https://github.com/Jinn-Network/spec#conformance-vectors): inputs paired with expected outcomes, as data. An implementation conforms to a family when it produces every expected outcome.
- **Propose a change.** Anything that is not a draft changes through a [numbered proposal](https://github.com/Jinn-Network/spec#proposing-a-change).

## What it does not yet show

One implementation exists, in TypeScript, in [Jinn-Network/mono](https://github.com/Jinn-Network/mono). No second implementation exists yet, so interoperability is a claim, not a result. The specification's [README](https://github.com/Jinn-Network/spec#readme) keeps the full list of what is still open.
