# Bullseye Framework — Operators, Contributors, Launchers

10 April 2026. Working framework, not a plan. Revisit after first loops.

## The Three Audiences

Each audience has a different motivation, a different channel, and a different conversion action. Don't collapse them into one funnel.

### 1. Operators
**Who:** Technical people who will run a Jinn node on testnet. Crypto-native, staking-literate, interested in earning. OLAS ecosystem is the warmest pool.
**Conversion action:** Running the client on testnet.
**Why they care:** Earn from their node's accumulated execution knowledge. Early positioning before mainnet.

### 2. Contributors
**Who:** Developers who want to build on/with Jinn. New clients, templates, integrations, protocol work. Found on GitHub, dev forums, crypto-infra communities.
**Conversion action:** A PR, an issue, a fork, a deployed integration.
**Why they care:** Open protocol, interesting problem (distributed execution memory), fair launch means early contributors matter more.

### 3. Launchers
**Who:** Non-technical (or semi-technical) people with deep domain knowledge who want to create agents around their expertise. The chess-obsessed accountant. The rare-disease researcher. Found everywhere but harder to reach without a polished product.
**Conversion action:** Creating an intent contract around their domain.
**Why they care:** Turn what they know into something that works autonomously and earns.

## Bullseye: Channel Brainstorm

Outer ring — everything plausible. Middle ring — worth testing cheaply. Inner ring — TBD (nothing's earned it yet).

### Contributors

| Ring | Channel | Why / How |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Middle | **GitHub discoverability** | Good README, labelled issues ("good first issue"), clear contribution guide. Zero cost. High signal. |
| Middle | **OLAS developer community** | Devs already building on OLAS stack. Jinn extends it. Direct outreach to active OLAS contributors. |
| Middle | **Hacker News / Reddit (r/ClaudeAI, r/LocalLLaMA)** | AI developers who feel the extraction problem. Show real work, not pitch decks. |
| Outer | **Hackathons / bounties** | Pay for specific contributions. Expensive per unit but produces real artifacts. |

### Launchers

| Ring | Channel | Why / How |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Outer | **Content marketing** (blog, Twitter) | "What if your obsession could earn?" stories. Needs a polished product to point to. |
| Outer | **Niche communities** (chess forums, medical research groups, wine communities) | Go where domain experts hang out. Very targeted, very manual. |
| Outer | **AI-curious professional communities** | LinkedIn, Substack ecosystem. People who read about AI disruption but don't know what to do about it. |
| Outer | **Partnerships with AI tool builders** | e.g. integrate with Claude Code, Cursor. Their users are potential launchers. Far future. |

**Note:** Launchers are mostly outer-ring right now. The product needs to be further along before this audience is actionable. Don't spread thin trying to reach them yet.

## Sequencing: What to Test First

The Bullseye rule: pick 3 channels, run cheap tests, measure, then focus.

**Right now (Phase 1a — testnet):**

1. **OLAS Discord / forums** — Operators. You're already known there. Post about testnet, DM active OLAS node operators directly. Measure: how many people DM back, how many run the client.

2. **GitHub discoverability** — Contributors. Clean up the mono repo README, label issues, write a contribution guide. Measure: forks, stars, PRs from people who aren't you.

3. **Hacker News / Reddit** — Contributors + Operators. Write one post about the execution memory thesis. Not a launch post — a "here's what we're building and why" post. Measure: comments, DMs, testnet signups.

That's it. Three channels. Run them. See what pulls.

## What "Running a Loop" Looks Like

Per channel, one loop = one cycle of: **do → measure → decide**.

Example loop for OLAS Discord:
1. **Do:** Post in OLAS Discord about Jinn testnet. DM 10 active node operators with a personal invite.
2. **Measure:** How many replied? How many ran the client? What questions did they ask?
3. **Decide:** Was this worth the time? What would make the next round better? Do another loop or move on.

A loop should take days, not weeks. If you're planning for longer than you're executing, stop planning.
